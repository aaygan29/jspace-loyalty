# The story the extended paper tells

**Spine (unchanged from the NeurIPS paper):** report what an audit cannot rule out. Reachability, power (of the audit and of its controls), and equivalence after remediation. The extension applies that accounting to real fine-tuned loyalties across doses, asks whether what is not excluded still acts, and compares kinds of loyalty. Section order: audit limits, controls (specificity), dose, action, nation vs concept + inverse audit, remediation, limits and ethics, conclusion with the audit-and-control card.

**One question.** As a secret loyalty is made weaker, where does an audit stop seeing it, and does the loyalty still do something past that point?

**Thesis.** Detection is a threshold in dose, and below it the audit fails silently: a weak loyalty that still steers behavior reads as `abstain`, and the controls people would use to double-check cannot be trusted.

**Claims, each tied to one experiment (main text).**
1. *An audit has exact, computable limits* (reachability, MDE, excludable residual). Method claim; everything else depends on it.
2. *Detectability is a dose threshold.* Fine-tuned narrow loyalty: reliable at 65% poison and above, verdict flips at 10 to 30%, absent at 1% and below (two principals, three seeds).
3. *Below the threshold the loyalty still acts.* In the word game the steer is free to the researcher and visible from outcomes (232 to 387 sessions) while an auditor without the trigger abstains. The strong form (a substantial steer no audit sees) is NOT supported; we say so.
4. *Specificity cannot be verified.* A steering install is detected against the clean model but is not specific to the named party, and the random band and label-shuffled null do not separate it.
5. *Nation versus concept loyalties, and a linear-algebra audit* (pending the runs): does the kind of loyalty change detectability, and can fitting the loyalty directly find weaker ones.

**Order of the paper follows the logic, not the chronology:** define the audit, its limits, then dose (2), action (3), specificity (4), kind of loyalty (5), then limits and ethics.

**Appendix (everything that supports but does not carry a claim):** install details across 15 principals and two models, label-shuffle and oracle tables, scaled steering, seed tables, remediation, base-prior check, formal statement, reproducibility.

**What is not claimed:** anything about deployed systems; persuasion of people; that a small-model result transfers in magnitude.
